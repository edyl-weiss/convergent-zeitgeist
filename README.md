Find the single cultural concept that connects all four clues. It could be a city, country, cultural movement, historical era, tradition, religion, artistic style, cuisine, trade network, or another recognisable cultural concept.

Clues arrive in order. Clues 1 and 2 start lit. Clue 1 is broad; clue 2 adds a new angle and may lead you astray; clue 3 narrows the field; clue 4 settles it. Guess any time. The light meter under the board shows what you'd score if your next guess is right.

Connections are either direct (Kaiseki → Kyoto) or run through one intermediate concept (a card game → Nintendo → Kyoto). Some clues honestly point to more than one answer. Red herrings are always real connections, never false facts.

Wrong guesses teach you something. If your guess fits some of the clues shown, those beams bend toward it for a moment. Type two letters for suggestions.

You get 5 guesses (4 in a Hard round) and one hint button you can press up to three times. The first hint names the answer's type and shows its letter spaces at the focal point; the second explains how the clues connect and fills in first letters; the third gives a place or period and fills in last letters.

Spot the red herring. After you solve, all four connections are shown. Pick the clue that could most easily have led somewhere else for a bonus.

The daily game is three rounds: Easy, Medium, then Hard, worth ×1, ×1.5 and ×2. Each round unlocks when you finish the one before, and solving all three adds a sweep bonus. When the library has no puzzle validated at a round's difficulty that day, the round uses the hardest one available a level down.

Endless serves puzzles back to back with 3 lives: three Easy, four Medium, then Hard. Each solve in a row adds 10% to your points, up to double. A miss costs a life and resets the combo. Endless never serves a puzzle from the coming week of daily games.
